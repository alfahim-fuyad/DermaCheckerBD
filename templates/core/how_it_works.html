How DermaCheckerBD works

From photo upload to optional follow-up, here is what to expect.

1. Take and upload a photo

Upload a clear JPG, PNG, or WEBP photo (up to 10 MB). On supported mobile browsers, you can take a photo directly.

Before continuing, the app checks that the image can be opened and used.

2. Check photo quality

The Smart Photo Guide checks for common issues with blur, lighting, and framing. If a photo may be hard to assess, it suggests taking another.

3. Review the experimental result

The app analyzes the photo and shows a predicted category with a confidence score. This is experimental information, not a diagnosis, and it can be wrong.

Read the limitations shown with the result and consult a qualified dermatologist if you have concerns.

4. Check result stability (optional)

The Reliability Check compares the result across small changes to the photo and shows how consistently the same category appears.

Lower agreement means the results varied across these checks; it does not measure medical correctness.

Even 100% agreement does not mean the prediction is correct.

5. Compare multiple photos (optional)

Compare results from several photos of the same lesion. The app highlights when results differ; a difference is not a diagnosis or proof that a lesion has changed.

6. Ask the assistant

Ask the on-page assistant about terms, confidence scores, and the app's limitations. It cannot diagnose or recommend treatment, and your uploaded image is not sent to the assistant.

7. Save observations (signed-in users)

Signed-in users can save photos and dates to a private follow-up timeline. This helps organize observations over time; it does not determine whether a condition has changed.

8. Doctor Panel (authorized users)

Authorized doctors and administrators can review follow-up cases, update review status, and generate serial reports. This supports follow-up workflows; it does not provide a diagnosis.

Important: DermaCheckerBD is experimental, is not medically validated, and cannot replace professional care. Photo comparisons can be affected by lighting, distance, angle, and scale. If a lesion is changing, bleeding, growing, or painful, consult a qualified dermatologist.
